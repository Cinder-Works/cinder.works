How this works

Sell now and Buy now come from Claude, an AI. It forecast each price from one week of prices, stock and sales. Where it expects a price 10% or more lower, that is a Sell: sell what you have, and wait if you need some. 8% or more higher is a Buy: buy what you need today, and hold what you have. A price that has simply fallen faster than Claude expected is not a Buy; it shows No call until the next forecast. Treat each as a lean, not a promise.

The bars are how much a call matters: the money at stake on each item (one bar under 1 silver, two up to 10 silver, three above). A bar comes off when Claude's own range is wide enough that the price could go the other way, and another when fewer than 20 sell a day. Things that sell fewer than 5 a day stay out of the top lists. If a price has jumped since the forecast was made, the call stays only when the item's own week agrees.

Craft for gold is plain arithmetic on today's prices: the cheapest listing of the finished item, less the auction house's 5% cut, less its materials at their going price. Only things that sell at least 5 a day. Recipes a trainer teaches come first; one that says "needs Pattern" (or Plans, Schematic, Recipe) is taught by that item instead, so it loses a bar. The listing deposit is not counted, and a profit this visible draws other crafters, so check in game before a big batch.

Badges on a chart mark things worth a look: the Darkmoon Faire opening and closing, a jump that held, a blip that was gone by the next scan (often just the price data hiccuping), the launch-week slide, a coming Saturday, and now and then a note Claude wrote after looking into an odd move. Point at one or tap it. Each is a guess read off the numbers, not a known cause.

Prices come from AHledger, which collects auction house scans from players' addons. Every update of this page is a fresh pull that reworks every call, bar and craft together; the top of the page says how old the prices are.

What is listed: the main things each profession buys on the auction house, while any are for sale. "Price each" is the middle price across everything listed; "cheapest" is the lowest single listing, the one you pay or have to undercut. Vials, thread, spices, flux and common dyes are left out because trade vendors sell them.
